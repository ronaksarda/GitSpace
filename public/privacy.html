What GitSpace reads from GitHub

GitSpace is a student project. Here is, in plain words, what happens with your data.

When you sign in with GitHub

We ask GitHub for no extra permissions, so we only get what is already public on your profile: your username, avatar, name, bio, location and your public repositories with their stats (stars, language, size, description, last update).

We cannot see your private repositories, and we never post, change or delete anything on your GitHub.

What we keep

  • Your username, avatar, main language, and when you first and last signed in.
  • For each public repo: its name, description, language, size, stars, whether it is a fork, its link and when it was last updated.
  • A login session cookie, so you stay signed in (it expires after 24 hours).
  • Your GitHub access token, stored encrypted, so we can refresh your island. It is deleted when you sign out or when the session expires.

Nothing is sold or shared with anyone.

Who can see your island

Anyone visiting GitSpace can see your island, because it is built only from public GitHub data.

The site also loads fonts from Google Fonts and avatars from GitHub, so those services see your visit the way they would on any website.

Removing your island

  1. Sign out of GitSpace.
  2. Revoke GitSpace's access at github.com/settings/applications.
  3. Open an issue at github.com/ronaksarda/GitSpace/issues and we will delete your island.
